This work implements and evaluates a heterogeneous \emph{mixture-of-experts} framework for sentiment analysis that combines Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRMs), classical machine-learning experts (TF--IDF with logistic regression or linear SVM), and deep learning / transformer encoders. Predictions are fused through stacking with five-fold out-of-fold meta-learning and through dense or sparse MoE gating. The thesis-scale protocol covers \textbf{four} public corpora (Sentiment140, IMDB, Amazon reviews, TweetEval Feminism), \textbf{35} trained model configurations, and \textbf{140} model--dataset pairs. The strongest stacking ensemble (\textbf{STACK1}) reaches macro-F1 \textbf{0.8911} on IMDB and \textbf{0.8746} on Amazon under the reported settings. Across all conditions, traditional ML achieves the highest \emph{mean} macro-F1 (\textbf{0.6389}), while transformers and HRMs average \textbf{0.3099} and \textbf{0.2502} respectively in this checkout---motivating ensemble design, imbalance-aware training, and architecture refinements. This paper also documents the live project tree: merged Parquet alignment, exported model and path inventories, regenerated charts, and a small HRAST per-stem evaluation slice for regression testing (not a second full benchmark). The emphasis is modular pipelines, reproducibility, and interpretability hooks rather than a new single-backbone classifier.
