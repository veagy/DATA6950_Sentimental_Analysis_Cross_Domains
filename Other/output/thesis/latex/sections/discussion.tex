\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discuss}

Section~\ref{sec:thesis_results} quantifies the thesis-scale aggregates; we interpret them here rather than restating tables. TF-IDF plus linear models remain competitive and anchor strong stacks~\cite{ref33,ref35}. STACK1 shows measurable gains on balanced, medium-length data when diverse experts are combined with a meta-learner. Transformer underperformance likely reflects tuning depth, sequence length, imbalance, and compute limits relative to corpus scale~\cite{ref03,ref34}. HRM mean macro-F1 suggests architecture--task mismatch or integration issues; the HRM-heavy STACK4 pattern reinforces caution. Sentiment140 highlights class imbalance and noise; sarcasm and figurative language remain difficult even for strong pipelines~\cite{ref36}. HRM levels and MoE expert weights still provide audit surfaces for interpretability~\cite{ref04,ref08}.

Aggregated macro-F1 across all experiment rows averages \textbf{0.4557} (std.\ 0.2187) with accuracy, precision, and recall exhibiting similar dispersion, reflecting the mixture of easy corpora (IMDB, Amazon) and hard social/stance tasks. Mean macro-F1 by dataset (IMDB $\approx$0.61, Amazon $\approx$0.58, Feminism $\approx$0.35, Sentiment140 $\approx$0.28) underscores that surface form and class priors dominate headline scores as much as architecture choice. Figures~\ref{fig:capstone_ml_b3} and~\ref{fig:perf_3label}  visualize binary vs.\ ternary difficulty and accuracy--F1 gaps on ancillary corpora; readers should treat them as qualitative complements to the thesis tables, especially when accuracy and macro-F1 diverge (a hallmark of imbalance).

\subsection{Limitations and threats to validity}
The thesis aggregates mix architectures with different training budgets; cross-domain rows are informative but not always directly comparable to in-domain optima. The HRAST stem slice (Section~\ref{sec:hrast}, Appendix~\ref{app:hrast}) uses small $n$ per model and must not be read as a second benchmark competing with IMDB or Amazon. Finally, reported CIs and significance tests (where present in the thesis) depend on the chosen seeds and folds; expanding seeds would tighten confidence in small gaps between ensembles.
