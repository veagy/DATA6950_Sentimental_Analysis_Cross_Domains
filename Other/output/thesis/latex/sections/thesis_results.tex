\section{Thesis-Scale Experimental Results}
\label{sec:thesis_results}

This section reproduces the thesis summary tables (Sections V-A--V-F of the implementation report) without restating methodology. Numeric values match that document. \subsection{Performance by model type}
Table~\ref{tab:by_type} breaks macro-F1 down by architectural family. Traditional ML (TF-IDF + linear models) achieves the highest mean macro-F1; transformers and HRMs underperform on average in the reported setup, motivating ensemble design and future architecture or training refinements.



\subsection{Performance by dataset}
Table~\ref{tab:by_dataset} orders corpora by mean macro-F1. IMDB and Amazon are comparatively moderate; TweetEval Feminism and especially Sentiment140 are harder under the thesis protocol, consistent with stance ambiguity and Twitter noise.



\subsection{Top ensembles and best singles}
Table~\ref{tab:top_ensemble} highlights STACK1 and reference singles. STACK1 reaches strong macro-F1 on balanced, longer reviews; Sentiment140 remains difficult even for the best single BERT-family expert.



\subsection{Ensemble comparison}
Table~\ref{tab:ensemble_cmp} contrasts average macro-F1 across ensemble families. STACK1 leads the reported averages, while STACK4 (HRM-heavy) is far weaker, underscoring that simply adding HRM experts without adequate training signal does not yield gains.



\subsection{Binary versus multi-class}
Table~\ref{tab:bin_mc} states the mean macro-F1 gap between binary and multi-class tasks in the thesis experiments.
