\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Thesis-scale aggregates (all headline corpora)}
Section~\ref{sec:thesis_results} reports \textbf{full-corpus} aggregates for the four public datasets in the  protocol (Sentiment140, IMDB, Amazon, TweetEval Feminism): global means, per model-type means, per-dataset means, ensemble comparisons, and binary vs.\ multi-class gaps. Those numbers reflect training and evaluation on the \textbf{complete} thesis splits, not a single stem slice.

\subsection{Repository HRAST slice (config-scan figures and appendix)}
\label{sec:hrast}
The charts and appendix table in this subsection use the \textbf{HRAST} row of \path{output/metrics/summary.csv} and \path{output/charts/metrics/...} because the automated config-scan was executed on that stem in this checkout (small $n$, smoke / regression purpose). This does \textbf{not} limit the scope of datasets you trained on: Table~\ref{tab:processed_stems} lists every processed Parquet stem (Amazon, IMDB, medical, patient, Sentiment140, TweetEval, Yelp, HRAST). Appendix~\ref{app:hrast} now lists only models with \emph{computable} metrics on HRAST (failed loads omitted from the table, still in the CSV).

Fig.~\ref{fig:rank2}--\ref{fig:paired} show macro-F1 rankings for two- and three-class modes and a paired comparison. Fig.~\ref{fig:heat_pair} contrasts metric heatmaps; Fig.~\ref{fig:eval_pair} shows evaluation status panels. Fig.~\ref{fig:labels_hrast}--\ref{fig:conf} give the HRAST label distribution and a 2-label RoBERTa confusion matrix.
